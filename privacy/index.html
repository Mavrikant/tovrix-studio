Privacy Policy

Orbit Stackers ("the game") is published by Tovrix Studio ("we"). Last updated: 26 November 2026.

What we collect

  • Guest account: when you first open the game, an anonymous account with a random identifier is created for your device so your progress can be saved. It is not connected to your name, email address or phone number, and a session token that keeps you signed in is stored on your device.
  • Email address (only if you sign in): if you choose Settings > Account > Sign in, we store your email address with your account so you can get your progress back on another phone, and send you one-time codes to sign in. It is used for nothing else: it is not shown to other players, not used for marketing, and not sent with gameplay statistics or crash reports. Signing in is optional; the game works without it.
  • Google account (only if you choose "Continue with Google"): you sign in with Google's own sign-in screen (on Android the phone's Google account sheet, elsewhere Google's page in your browser), and Google tells our sign-in provider the email address, name and profile picture address of your Google account. They are stored with your account by the sign-in provider. The game uses only the email address, for the same purpose as above; it does not read your name or picture, does not show any of this to other players, and does not send it with gameplay statistics or crash reports. The game never sees your Google password and gets no access to your Google account, contacts or files.
  • Nickname: the pilot name you choose. It is visible to other players in matches and on the leaderboard, and to your friends. Please do not use your real name. The game refuses names with insults or slurs, and shows such a name from before as "Pilot" to other players.
  • Name reports (online play only): when you report a name on the leaderboard as offensive, we store that your account reported that name of that player, and when. The reported player is not told who reported them. When several players have reported the same name, it is replaced by a neutral one.
  • Friends (online play only): a friend code that the game creates for you; who your friends are; friend requests that are waiting, were declined or were blocked, with the code that was typed for them; the time of the last invite to play between two friends and whether it has been seen; whether you muted a friend's invites; and whether you accept friend requests. Your friend code is visible to anyone you give it to and to anyone you send a friend request to. Your nickname, trophies and league are shown as a friend only to players whose request you accepted or who accepted yours; a player who only sent you a request, or received one from you, sees a friend code and nothing else. An invite is a fixed notice (that you are playing, with a button to join the ranked queue) with no text of your own. No contact list, phone number or device identifier is used to find friends.
  • Invite notifications (Android app, online play, only while Settings > Friend invites is on, you have at least one friend and you have allowed notifications for the game): so that a friend's invite can reach you while the game is closed, the game stores with your account a push token (an address for notifications that Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging gives your installation of the game), the language of your game, and the time of the last notification we sent you. A player without friends has no push token: the game asks Google for one only when you have a friend. See "Sharing" for what Google receives.
  • Gameplay data: trophies, coins, gems, cards, deck, shop purchases made with in-game currency (including the daily, weekly and monthly deals and the free gift of the day), Orbit Pass progress, daily mission progress, achievements, tower skins, match results and the times you last played.
  • Technical data: your IP address is processed while your device is connected to our backend, as is necessary for any internet connection. The game's own database does not store it. Our backend provider, Supabase, records it with your anonymous sign-in and keeps standard access logs for at most 7 days.
  • Crash and error reports: when the game crashes or hits an error, it sends a report with the error message, the place in the program where it happened, the game version, and your device model and operating-system version. The report is not connected to your account identifier or nickname. In the Android app, when the app itself crashes, and once each time it starts, the crash reporter also includes a random installation identifier that it creates on your device; it is reset when you reinstall the game. Reports are received by our error-reporting provider, Sentry (Functional Software, Inc., a US company), stored in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany) and kept for at most 90 days. Sentry processes your IP address when a report arrives and derives an approximate location (country and city) from it.
  • Gameplay statistics: to see how the game is played, balance it and find problems, the game sends statistics about your matches: how a match went (ranked or practice, against a player or an AI, online or offline, the result and how it ended, how long it took, tower heights, your league and the trophy change), how many cards were played and which ones, how many modules you placed and lost, how long you waited for a rival, card upgrades and deck changes, whether you started, skipped or completed the welcome screen and the training flight, connection problems and errors of the game service, together with the game version, your device model and operating-system version. The game also sends roughly how long ago you first played on this device and roughly how long it has been since you last opened the game, each only as a range of days (for example "2 to 6 days" or "30 days or more"), never as a date or a time. And it sends when you claim a daily bonus: that the daily sign-in bonus was claimed and which day of its 7-day cycle it was, or that a win brought the bonus for the first win of the day, without the coins or cards you received. To work out the two ranges, the game keeps two dates on your device: when it was first started and when it was last started. They are kept there whether or not statistics are switched on, and they are never sent to us or to anyone else. This is switched off by default: nothing is sent unless you switch it on in the game under Settings > Share gameplay statistics. You can switch it off again at any time, and nothing more is sent from that moment. The statistics are not connected to your account identifier or nickname, and no identifier is created for them, so we cannot tell which player or which device a match came from. They are received by Sentry (Functional Software, Inc., a US company), stored in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany) and kept for at most 90 days. Sentry processes your IP address when the statistics arrive and derives an approximate location (country and city) from it.

Daily reminder (Android app): the game can show one notification a day, around 19:00, to remind you to play on days you have not opened it yet. The reminder is scheduled on your device by the game itself: no push service is used, nothing is sent to us or to anyone else for it, and it contains no personal data. On Android 13 and later the game asks for permission to show notifications once, after your first finished match (or, if you had not been asked yet, when you switch on Settings > Friend invites). The same permission covers invite notifications; the game does not ask a second time. You can switch the reminder off at any time in the game under Settings > Daily reminder, or in your phone's notification settings.

Rewarded ads (Android app): you can choose to watch an ad to add a flight to the charging crate. Ads are never shown unless you tap "Watch an ad", and never during a match. The first time you tap it, the game starts Google's ad service (Google Mobile Ads / AdMob, Google LLC) and, where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), shows Google's consent form, where you choose whether ads may be personalised; you can consent, not consent, or choose per purpose. In US states with their own privacy laws it shows a form where you can opt out of the sale or sharing of your data for ads. Google then collects, for showing and measuring the ad and for preventing fraud, your device's advertising ID, your IP address, information about your device and app, and how you interact with the ad, as described in Google's policy at https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. You can change your choice under Settings > Ad privacy choices (shown where the consent form applies), and reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings. The game sends Google nothing about your account, nickname or progress. If you never tap "Watch an ad", the ad service is not started.

Apart from those optional ads, the game does not include advertising or advertising trackers, and does not access your contacts, photos, location, microphone or camera. Settings such as language, sound and the daily reminder, and the two dates on which the game was first and last started, are stored only on your device. In offline mode nothing is sent to us at all, apart from crash and error reports and, if you switched them on, gameplay statistics.

Why we use it

To run the game: saving your progress, matching you with rivals, showing leaderboards and preventing abuse. We do this because it is needed to provide the game you asked for (performance of a contract), and to keep it fair and safe for all players (our legitimate interests). To find and fix errors, on the basis of our legitimate interests in a working, fair and safe game. To understand how the game is played so that we can balance and improve it, through gameplay statistics, only with your consent: you give it by switching on Settings > Share gameplay statistics and withdraw it at any time by switching it off. Neither crash reports nor gameplay statistics are connected to your account or nickname.

Sharing

We do not sell your data. It is processed on our behalf by our backend provider, Supabase (Supabase, Inc.), which stores it in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany) and, for crash and error reports and gameplay statistics, by Sentry (Functional Software, Inc., a US company), which stores them in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany). If you choose to watch a rewarded ad, Google (Google LLC) receives the data described under "Rewarded ads" as an independent controller. If you choose "Continue with Google", Google (Google Ireland Limited or Google LLC, depending on where you live) learns that you signed in to this game with your Google account, under its own privacy policy; the game sends Google nothing about your play.

If you buy the VIP subscription or an item in the shop's offers (gems, coins, upgrade packs, the starter pack, the colour pack), the purchase is made with Google Play and handled by Google under its own terms. For the subscription we receive from Google whether it is active and until when, and store that date with your account; for an item we receive whether it was paid and store a short code derived from the purchase (a one-way hash), so that it is granted only once, and which once-only items your account bought. To tie a purchase to your game account we give Google a code derived from your account identifier (a one-way hash), not your name or email address. We do not receive your payment details.

If invite notifications are on, the notification is delivered by Firebase Cloud Messaging, a service of Google (Google Ireland Limited or Google LLC). For each notification Google receives your push token and a fixed text in your language ("A friend invites you to a match. Open the game to play!"). It receives nothing about the friend who invited you and nothing about your account: no nickname, friend code or identifier of either player. We send at most one such notification in five minutes, and none for anything but a friend's invite. To give your installation a push token, the Firebase software inside the Android app contacts Google when invite notifications are first switched on for you (not before, and never if you have no friends): Google then receives what it needs to issue the token, such as an identifier of the installation (Firebase installation ID), the app's identifier and your IP address, under Google's own privacy policy. The game uses no other Firebase or Google service for this: no analytics and no advertising identifier. We may disclose data if the law requires it.

Retention and deletion

Game accounts are not deleted for inactivity: your progress stays until you delete your account (see below). Name reports are kept for 30 days, and are deleted earlier with the account of either player. Friend data is kept until you remove the friend, cancel the request or unblock the player, or until you delete your account. Requests that were not answered are removed 14 days after they were sent, the next time either of the two players adds a friend. A request you declined is remembered for the same time, so that the same player cannot keep asking; so is a request from a player you blocked and then unblocked while it was still waiting. The push token for invite notifications is deleted, with us and at Google, when you switch off Settings > Friend invites, when you remove your last friend, when you withdraw the notification permission (the next time the game is opened online) and when you sign out; we also delete it when Google reports that the game was removed from your phone, and with your account. You can delete your account and all of its data at any time, in the game or by email: see "Deleting your account" below. Crash reports and gameplay statistics are not stored with your account and cannot be found by account or nickname, so deleting the account does not remove them; they are deleted automatically after the period given above. To stop gameplay statistics, switch off Settings > Share gameplay statistics.

Deleting your account

This is how to delete your Orbit Stackers account (published by Tovrix Studio) and the data that belongs to it. You do not need to keep the game installed for the second way.

  1. In the game: open Settings, tap Delete account and confirm. The account is deleted at once, with everything listed under "What we collect" that is stored with it: your progress, nickname, friends, friend requests and blocks (you also disappear at once from other players' lists), name reports made by you or about you, the push token, the record of your purchases and VIP subscription, and the email address or Google account details if you had signed in.
  2. By email: write to tovrixstudio@gmail.com with the subject "Delete my Orbit Stackers account".
    • If you had signed in with an email address or with Google, send it from that address.
    • For a guest account, give the pilot name and the friend code shown in the game. To make sure the account is yours, we answer with a short word and ask you to set it as your pilot name in Settings; we then delete the account.

    We delete the account within 30 days of the request and confirm it by email. We cannot delete a guest account that we cannot tell apart from other players' accounts.

What is not deleted: crash reports and gameplay statistics, which are not stored with your account and cannot be found by account or nickname (they expire on their own, see "Retention and deletion"), and the records that Google keeps of purchases made with Google Play, under Google's terms.

Children

The game is meant for players aged 13 and over and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has created an account, email tovrixstudio@gmail.com and we will delete it.
